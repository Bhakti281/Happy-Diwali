Chapter One

The Festival
of Lights

दीप

On the darkest night of the year, an entire country chooses light. Not the harsh light of noon — something gentler: clay lamps filled with oil, a wick, a wish, set on doorsteps until the streets themselves seem to breathe.

Diwali is older than memory. It is the homecoming of Rama to Ayodhya, welcomed by a city of lamps. It is the day Lakshmi is invited across the threshold. It is the moment when we are asked to remember that darkness is not defeated by shouting — it is simply lit.

So this is not a greeting card. It is a courtyard at night, waiting for you to light it.

Light over darkness

Every flame is a small defiance — the oldest reminder that one lamp can end a long night.

Homecoming

Homes are washed, thresholds drawn with rangoli, and every door opens a little wider.

Abundance, shared

Sweets passed between neighbours, gifts given without keeping score, sweetness made communal.
